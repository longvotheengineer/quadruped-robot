\section{Results and Diagnostic Data}

A comprehensive diagnostic pipeline continuously broadcasts
internal controller states across a structured topic hierarchy
to objectively quantify algorithmic performance. Data is presented
separately for the static home posture and dynamic trotting gait.

\subsection{Static Home Posture Stabilization}

During the static home posture, the quadruped maintains all four
limbs in ground contact while the PID controller actively
rejects base orientation disturbances.

Fig.~\ref{fig:home_results} presents the pitch and roll error tracking response. The robust orientation error is shown to converge rapidly, settling completely within the configured $\pm0.02$ rad deadband threshold in approximately $1.5$ seconds post-disturbance. This empirical convergence confirms that the deadband and integral anti-windup mechanisms effectively stabilize the system. The multistage pipeline produces appropriate, critically-damped actuator commands suitable for sustained balancing without triggering unwanted limit-cycle oscillations around the spatial setpoint.

\subsection{Dynamic Trotting Gait Stabilization}

During active trotting locomotion, the adaptive PID controller
maintains postural stability while the quadruped executes cyclic
quintic trajectories.

Fig.~\ref{fig:trot_results} displays the dynamic pitch and roll error during the trotting phase. Despite the periodic ground impact forces inherently generating transient orientation spikes of up to $\sim 0.15$ rad, the adaptive mechanisms correctly extract the sustained tilt trend from the severe cyclic gait artifacts. The phase-aware PID controller successfully binds the peak steady-state tracking error within the rigid $0.08$ rad dynamic threshold defined by the proportional gain schedule. This decisive corrective response mitigates external dynamic shifts and effectively prevents long-term drift accumulation, ensuring steady, continuous planar ambulation.
